# S&P 500 band width from a live VIX feed — 2026-11-05

Verdict: **promoted**. The S&P 500 band width uses VIX implied variance for the
first 365 days. The median is unchanged. The quote worker and D1 are not deployed
yet; that deploy needs owner approval.

## What changed

The spot variance comes from the VIX close of the session before the origin:

```
spot  = (VIX / 100)^2 / 252
scale = c * sqrt(spot)          first 365 days, c = 0.7
```

A stale VIX is never carried forward. When the feed has no fresh close the model
falls back to the price-only EWMA term structure. Past 365 days it adds the
price-only term-structure increments.

`src/lib/marketForecast.ts` adds
`SP500_VIX_TERM_STRUCTURE = { horizonDays: 365, persistence: 0.998, longRunDailyVol: 0.0106, scale: 0.7 }`.

Data path:

- `workers/market-quote-refresh` refreshes `^VIX` into D1 as series `vix`.
- `/api/market-data` returns `impliedVolRows` for S&P 500; the client merges them.
- Bundled `src/data/vix-history.json` (2010-09-09 to 2026-11-04), written by
  `scripts/update-market-data.mjs`.

## Method

- Research fit the parameters on origins before 2019 only: ^GSPC 1991-2018. The
  score is the pinball loss over q05/q10/q50/q90/q95 at 7/14/30/90/180/365 days.
- `npm run backtest:market-production` is the gate. It runs the production
  `buildGenericModelInputs` and `computeGenericProbabilityForecast` every 5th
  session from the 1000th row. Train is before 2019, holdout is 2019+. The
  challenger arm is VIX; the incumbent arm is the price-only EWMA term structure
  on the same inputs. t statistics are Newey-West.
- Gate: no holdout horizon loses pinball, mean gain at least 1%, and 90%
  coverage within 85-95% or no farther from 90% than the incumbent. All three
  gates pass: S&P 500 vs legacy, S&P 500 vs priceOnly, gold vs legacy.

## Caveats

- The S&P 500 CSR residual trees were fit on labels through 2026-01-01, so the
  S&P median is in-sample on this window. Both arms use the same median, so the
  width comparison is isolated.
- Train at 180 days is negative (−3.0%, t = −1.12). The holdout gain at that
  horizon is positive but weak (t = 1.86).
- VIX with c = 0.70 is too narrow at long horizons: research on the pre-2019 fit
  shows −2.6% at 730 days and −7.4% at 1,825 days, with 90% coverage at 68%.
  This is why VIX applies to the first 365 days only.
- Overlapping horizons make the effective sample smaller than n. The t statistics
  account for this; the coverage percentages do not.
- The worker and D1 are not deployed yet. Production shows the price-only fallback
  until the owner approves the deploy.
